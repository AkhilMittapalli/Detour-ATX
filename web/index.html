DetourATX

Austin publishes every road closure in the city and a detour for none of them. Put in two addresses and this shows what is actually broken on the way, how much of it can be trusted, and what to do about it.

Why not just use a maps app

A maps app sees the jam. This sees the paperwork.

Congestion data is built from phones already stuck in traffic, so it is reactive by construction — it knows about the queue once the queue exists. Permits, signal-controller state and closure records exist before anyone sets out, and no mapping vendor ingests them.

It tells you what is broken, not just what is slow.

A dark traffic signal is legally an all-way stop in Texas and most drivers do not know it. A permitted closure has an end date. A sidewalk shut for a curb ramp is invisible to a driving app and decisive if you use a wheelchair.

It admits what it does not know.

Of Austin’s work-zone records the city has verified a position on none. Every item here carries a confidence label, and the app would rather say “this may already be finished” than alarm you with a closure that is not there.

It answers questions about later, not only now.

Because permits carry dates, this can tell you what your route looks like tomorrow morning, or whether the thing blocking you clears next week. Live traffic data cannot answer that at any price: the congestion does not exist yet.
